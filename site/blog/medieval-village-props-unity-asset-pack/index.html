Medieval Village Props: A Game-Ready Unity Asset Pack for $9.99

Building a medieval village scene in Unity takes far more than houses and walls. It's the clutter that sells it: barrels by the tavern door, crates stacked in the market, ingots cooling by the forge, bread on the stall. Modeling all of that yourself eats weeks you could spend on gameplay.

Medieval Village Props is our answer: 149 game-ready meshes for medieval villages, markets, smithies, and taverns for $9.99. It's built from the same pipeline we use for our own medieval game, Omnivael: Wayfarer's Life.

What's inside

Version 2.1 of the pack includes 100 props plus 49 modular parts, organized into categories you'll actually use when dressing a scene:

  • Containers
  • Farm tools
  • Food
  • Market goods
  • Ingots
  • Ores
  • Smithy
  • Storage
  • Tavern
  • Timber

Every prop is shown in preview sheets on the product page, so you can see exactly what you're getting before you buy.

Built to drop into your scene

The pack is designed to save you setup time, not create more of it:

  • Real-world scale. Props sit correctly next to each other and your characters without rescaling.
  • 2K PBR textures: base colour, normal, and metallic/smoothness maps.
  • One material per prop, which keeps things simple to manage.
  • Prefabs with colliders, ready to place.
  • A demo scene with every prop laid out by category, so you can browse the whole set in-engine.

Modular fills: one prop, many looks

The feature we're proudest of is the modular fill system. It lets a small number of meshes tell a lot of different stories:

  • Modular open crates with 16 contents, in half or full fills
  • Barrels with adjustable liquid
  • Bottles and vials with fill levels

A half-empty crate of apples in one corner of the market, a full one at the next stall, a barrel nearly drained behind the tavern bar: small variations like these make a scene feel used and lived in instead of copy-pasted.

Engine and pipeline compatibility

  • Tested in Unity 6000.3 (Built-in render pipeline).
  • URP supported via Unity's material converter.

How it was made

We believe in being upfront about this. Some meshes in the pack were created with the help of AI tools (LCM Dreamshaper v7 reference images and Microsoft TRELLIS, MIT license), then cleaned, retopologised, re-textured, and checked by hand. Others are procedurally modelled in Blender. Full details are in the README included with the pack.

The hand pass is the important part. Raw AI-generated meshes aren't game-ready; ours have been reworked so they behave like the rest of the set.

Quick tips for dressing a village with the pack

  • Start with the demo scene. Browse every category in-engine and drag out the props you need for each area.
  • Zone by purpose. Put ores, ingots and smithy props together near the forge, food and goods in the market, and bottles and barrels around the tavern. Grouping props by what a place does makes it read instantly.
  • Vary your fills. Mix half and full crates and different liquid levels so repeated props don't look copy-pasted.
  • Stack storage and timber along walls and paths to add density without blocking movement. The colliders are already set up on the prefabs.
  • Leave breathing room. A few empty spots make the busy areas feel busier.
