# POS and Self-Order Kiosk Tips for Small Shops

If you run a small shop, your counter is where the day is won or lost. A slow register line costs you sales, a confusing menu costs you upsells, and a messy sales record makes reordering a guessing game. The good news: you don't need an enterprise system to fix any of it. You need a point-of-sale (POS) setup sized to how you actually sell, and, for some shops, a self-order kiosk that takes pressure off the counter.

Here are practical tips we'd give any single-location shop owner before they buy anything.

## 1. Start with how you actually ring up sales

Before comparing systems, spend one busy day watching your own counter. Write down:

- How many transactions happen in your peak hour
- How many items are in a typical sale
- How customers usually pay (card, tap, cash)
- What slows things down: price lookups, custom orders, making change, waiting for a card reader

A neighborhood coffee shop with 60 quick orders an hour has very different needs from a gift boutique with 15 slow, browsing-heavy sales a day. The coffee shop needs speed and modifiers ("oat milk, extra shot"). The boutique needs easy item lookup and a clear record of what sold. Pick the system that solves *your* bottleneck, not the one with the longest feature list.

## 2. Keep your item list clean and short

The biggest POS slowdown in small shops isn't the hardware. It's a cluttered item list. Tips:

- **Group items into a handful of clear categories** (e.g., Drinks, Food, Retail) so staff tap two buttons, not scroll ten screens.
- **Put your top 10 sellers on the first screen.** Most shops sell the same few items most of the day.
- **Use consistent names.** "Lg Latte" and "Latte – Large" as separate buttons will wreck your sales reports.
- **Retire items you no longer sell.** Old buttons cause mistakes.

## 3. Make card payments fast

Customers expect to tap and go. Make sure your card reader sits where the customer can reach it without stretching across the counter, and test the full payment flow before opening day, including refunds. A refund you can't figure out while a customer waits is a bad moment for everyone.

## 4. Use your sales record, not just your register

A POS isn't only for taking money. Even a simple record of "what went out the door" answers questions every owner has:

- What sold best this week, and what didn't move?
- Which hours are busiest, so you can schedule staff?
- When do you need to reorder your best sellers?

Set a weekly habit: ten minutes every Monday looking at last week's sales. Small shops that do this reorder smarter and waste less.

## 5. Know when a self-order kiosk makes sense

A self-order kiosk lets customers order, check in, or look up a menu without waiting on the counter. It's a strong fit when:

- **You have a line at peak times** and customers leave instead of waiting
- **Your menu has lots of options** (sizes, add-ons, combos) that take time to explain
- **Staff are stretched**, and freeing one person from order-taking lets them prep, restock, or help other customers
- **You handle check-ins**, like appointments or pickups, that don't need a person every time

It's a weaker fit if most of your sales are high-touch, like custom gifts or advice-heavy products, where the conversation *is* the sale.

## 6. Design the kiosk screen for a first-time customer

If you do add a kiosk, assume every customer has never seen it before:

- **Big buttons and photos.** People order faster when they can see the item.
- **Three taps to a basic order.** Pick item, pick options, pay.
- **Suggest one add-on, not five.** "Add a cookie?" works. A wall of upsells feels like a trap.
- **Clear "start over" and "help" buttons.** Nobody should feel stuck.
- **Place it where the line naturally forms**, with the counter still visible, so customers who prefer a person can still go there.

## 7. Keep the kiosk and the counter in sync

The worst kiosk experience is ordering something that's sold out. Whatever system you choose, your kiosk menu and your counter should show the same items and prices. When you 86 an item at the counter, it should disappear from the kiosk too.

## 8. Plan for the bad day

Internet drops. Printers jam. Cards get declined. Before you rely on any system, know:

- What happens if the internet goes down mid-rush?
- Who do you call when something breaks, and how fast do they answer?
- Can you still take cash and record the sale?

A local provider you can actually reach is worth a lot on a bad Saturday.

## 9. Try before you buy

Never commit to a POS or kiosk based on a brochure. Ask for a working demo with your own items on it. Ring up a few real orders. Have your busiest staff member try it. If it feels slow in the demo, it will feel slower on a Friday night.

## 10. Connect the counter to the rest of your business

Your POS is one piece. Customers also find you online and call you on the phone. A simple, fast website with your hours and menu and a phone line that always gets answered round out the picture, so the customer who finds you on Google actually makes it to your counter.
